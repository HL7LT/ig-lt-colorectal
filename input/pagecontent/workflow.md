# Colorectal cancer screening workflow

The colorectal cancer screening workflow begins when an eligible patient participates in the prevention program and performs a [fecal occult blood test (FOBT)](Observation-observation-colorectal-fobt-qualitative-not-detected-example.html). The test is ordered and documented as a laboratory observation to detect hidden blood in stool, which may indicate colorectal neoplasia.

A stool sample is collected and sent to the laboratory as a [specimen](Specimen-specimen-colorectal-fobt-example.html). The laboratory performs the analysis and records the results as a structured observation. If the result is negative, the patient continues routine screening at the recommended interval. If the result is positive, the patient is referred for further diagnostic evaluation.

The next step is a [screening colonoscopy](Procedure-procedure-colorectal-colonoscopy-no-anesthesia-example.html). During this procedure, the endoscopist examines the colon and rectum using a colonoscope and documents the extent of the examination, including the anatomical segment reached. The quality of bowel preparation is also assessed and recorded using the Boston bowel preparation scale.

During the colonoscopy, the endoscopist may identify pathological findings such as polyps, tumors, diverticula, or inflammatory changes. Each finding is documented together with its anatomical location, size, and morphological characteristics.

If a suspicious lesion or polyp is detected, the endoscopist may perform a biopsy or remove the lesion using a polypectomy. The collected tissue is placed in a container and recorded as a specimen, ensuring correct identification and traceability.

The specimen is transported to the pathology laboratory where it undergoes a histological examination. The pathologist performs a macroscopic evaluation of the material and prepares the tissue for microscopic analysis.

During microscopic examination, the pathologist determines the nature of the lesion and documents the findings as structured observations, including the histological diagnosis and other relevant pathological features.

Finally, all endoscopic and pathological findings are compiled into a comprehensive diagnostic report. The final document composition includes colonoscopy findings, specimen information, histological results, and the final diagnosis. The report is reviewed and signed by the responsible specialist and communicated back to the referring clinician, completing the colorectal cancer screening and diagnostic workflow.
